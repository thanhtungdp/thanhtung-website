## Product–Market Fit is a chain of evidence

Startups do not discover PMF in a meeting room. The founder goes to market, sells a promise, delivers value, watches customers return, and repeats until the business has a model clear enough to scale.

## Sell early to learn fast

Every transaction should produce four outputs: one new insight about the pain, one documented objection, one tested pricing hypothesis, and one standardized delivery step. A buying decision is the hardest signal to fake.

## Grow through leverage, not headcount

Strong partners bring networks, domain credibility and delivery capabilities the startup does not need to own yet. The core team focuses on the product, data and delivery standard, while expansion costs follow real opportunities and revenue.

## Systemize before scaling

Turn founder knowledge into a clear promise, sales and delivery playbooks, a shared data platform, accountable roles and a cadence for reading customer signals. Hire only after the bottleneck repeats, the winning method is clear and the unit economics can support it.
